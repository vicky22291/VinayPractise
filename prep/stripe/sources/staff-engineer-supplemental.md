# **Interviews and Stripe’s Operating Principles**

## Stripe’s operating principles guide how we interact with each other and our users, translating our values and beliefs into concrete actions. The principles articulated here detail the everyday behaviors we focus on, our core traits, and what we expect of our leaders—and all are rooted in the core expectation that Stripes operate with high integrity, maturity, and respect for others at all times.

## **Operating Principles**

* **Users first.** We serve millions of businesses and a meaningful fraction of global GDP. We have a weighty responsibility to those businesses and to society. We work backwards from our users’ needs, indexing especially on feedback from the most innovative. Everyone at Stripe talks to users.

* **Create with craft and beauty.** With careful thought, anything can be made surprisingly great. Across every part of Stripe, we are committed to doing so. Well-crafted work indicates care for the user, and beautiful work indicates care for the world.

* **Move with urgency and focus.** We move with speed on what matters most and take the time to invest in what will make us faster tomorrow. Whereas many companies get slower until they’re eventually mired in mediocrity, we aspire to become the world’s fastest company.

* **Collaborate egolessly.** We work as one team: no fiefdoms, no hoarding information, no “not my problem”. We question assumptions, debate **energetically**, and abandon ideas when better ones emerge. We’re generous with credit and stingy with blame. We learn from mistakes together.

* **Obsess over talent.** It’s every Stripe’s responsibility to help hire the best and to push for excellence everywhere. Managers must relentlessly uphold our high talent bar, lead their teams with clarity and context, foster development, and ultimately support Stripes in accomplishing the best work of their careers.

* **Stay curious.** Stripe is an applied exercise in learning about how businesses tick and how the world works. Since the details and possibilities are infinite, we are always seeking to learn. We’re energized by the unfamiliar, preferring the joy of discovery to the comfort of certainty.

## **Behavioral Interviewing with STAR**

*Wherever you see ‘Operating Principle Interview’ referenced as part of the interview process, you should use the following framework and tips to guide your response.* 

**Situation/Task:** explain the situation or task so the interviewer understands the context of your example

**Action:** provide details on what you (and/or a teammate, partner, or stakeholder) did to address the situation or carry out the task

**Result:** describe what your actions achieved and the ways in which you were effective, successful, unsuccessful, impactful, transformative, notable, etc.

## **Tips:**

* Be detailed, yet concise. Interviewers will ask follow-up questions when they need more details, but it’s best to stick to the STAR format in order to cover the who, what, where, why, and how behind your response  
* Never feel obligated to share details, metrics, people, or initiatives that are confidential or proprietary. If you need to redact sensitive details from your response, just explain this to the interviewer  
* Use “I” instead of “we” when speaking in first person about something that you individually owned, a decision that you individually made, or other instances where it’s important to clarify this to the interviewer. “We” can be used to represent the work of the broader team or a group of colleagues.   
* Specific examples help interviewers get a clear picture of who you are and how you work. Avoid generalizations (“this happens a lot, so I usually…”) or hypotheticals (“I would…”)  
* Try not to repeat examples; the more variety you share, the better your interviewers will understand the complexity and scope of your work  
* Ask clarifying questions. Interviewers are happy to make sure you understand what is being asked or re-frame a question that may have been unclear

## **Technical Interviews**

In these interviews, you’ll consider technical problems and then write working programs that solve them. In some cases, you’ll also have a design discussion with your interviewer before diving into coding. Our technical interviews simulate the engineering work you’d do day-to-day at Stripe. 

We want you to use whichever programming language, environment, and editor you’re most comfortable with. Please choose a language that you are fluent with, as some questions ask you to work with a mature codebase, and choosing a language you can’t write easily will make it hard to correctly assess your abilities. If you are using an unfamiliar IDE or runtime environment, take some time prior to the interview to acquaint yourself with how to run and debug code. 

We encourage you to use online resources (docs, Stack Overflow, etc.) as needed during the interview. **Please note that the use of any AI tools (e.g., Copilot, Gemini, TabNine, ChatGPT, LLMs, or any other AI assistants) in solving interview problems is strictly prohibited. This includes the use of AI results in search tools, such as Gemini for Google Search.**  

Consider your interviewer a collaborator on the problem. We suggest "working out loud" with your interviewer and asking any questions that you have as they come up, for example about constraints on the system.

#### HOW TO PREPARE

* You will need to use a computer (Mac, Windows, or Linux) that is set up for software development. You should not use your current employer’s computer.  
* We ask questions that require writing, running, and debugging code in your language of choice on your computer. You should have:   
  * A text editor or IDE you are comfortable with  
  * The ability to run a program and print its output  
  * We recommend completing the steps in “Environment Setup” below.   
* While your previous interview may have used an online coding platform such as HackerRank or CoderPad, the questions we ask during the virtual onsite will require compiling, editing, and running an open-source project on your computer. We encourage using debugging tools, built-in data structures, and any open source libraries/techniques at your disposal. Again, we strongly recommend cloning and executing the repositories in “Environment Setup”.  
* One of the interview questions will be structured around HTTP requests. You should have a working familiarity of an HTTP client of your choice in the language you are interviewing in. You should expect to be making HTTP requests to a public API and making use of the responses. A high-level understanding of [this article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertext_Transfer_Protocol#Request_methods) is a good reference.  
* We use the interview process to assess your own knowledge, communication skills, and problem-solving abilities. For these reasons, please do not use any AI writing assistants, chatbots, or copilot tools during the interview process, including written projects, unless otherwise agreed in writing with Stripe.   
* You may, of course, research Stripe to better understand our business, but please apply your own skills, experiences, and thought processes to respond to our questions and complete any assigned tasks. Using AI tools without Stripe’s agreement may disqualify your application.

## **AI-Enabled Programming Exercise** 

This interview evaluates your ability to solve real engineering problems by working effectively with AI tools. We want to understand how you apply AI in your workflow, not just whether you can prompt it, but how you plan, direct, validate, and build on what it produces.

#### WHAT TO EXPECT

You will complete a 60-minute, multi-part programming exercise in HackerRank's AI Coding Environment. You will have \~10 minutes for an intro and setup, then 45 minutes to solve the problem. The test environment will include an in-IDE agent like VS Code, Copilot, or Cursor agents, which is built directly into the HackerRank IDE, and you are actively encouraged to use it.

The problem is progressive and multi-part: your interviewer will reveal new sections as you work through the exercise, so you won't see the full problem up front.

#### HOW TO PREPARE

* **Use an AI coding assistant before your interview.** IDE sidebar agents like Cursor or GitHub Copilot are similar in feel to what you'll use on the day. Practice prompting with specific context, reviewing AI output critically, and iterating when it doesn't work as expected.  
* **Plan before you prompt.** Take a moment to understand the problem and sketch the shape of a solution before engaging the AI. Candidates who arrive at the AI with a clear intent — and explicit constraints — consistently get better results than those who prompt it cold.  
* **Think out loud.** Your interviewer wants to understand how you think. Narrate your reasoning when deciding how or why to prompt the AI, and when you're evaluating or pushing back on what it gives you.  
* **Actively review AI output.** Successful Stripes don't just accept what the agent gives them — they scrutinise the architecture, push back on over-engineered solutions, and iterate when something doesn't look right.  
* **Be ready to prove correctness.** At the end of the session, your interviewer will ask: "How do you know this is correct?" Think about how you'll demonstrate that — through test cases, manual verification, or reasoning about edge cases.  
* **Clarify when needed.** You can ask your interviewer for clarification at any point, just as you would in a standard interview.

#### WHAT WE EVALUATE FOR THIS INTERVIEW

* **System Architecting.** Do you envision, design, and iterate on a correct high-level structure for the solution, in partnership with the AI? Do you give the AI clear constraints and push back when it over-engineers or misses the mark?  
* **Critical Oversight.** Do you adopt an active and critical mindset towards AI-generated output? Can you identify "AI slop," requirements mismatches, or logical flaws — and do you know when to take ownership and work independently of the AI?  
* **Correctness & Debugging.** Do you demonstrate a strong sense of correctness and drive a systematic troubleshooting process? Do you produce good test cases, critically review AI suggestions, and retain technical independence when AI output misses the mark?  
* **Interaction & Collaboration.** Do you clearly explain your thought process, respond constructively to feedback, and ask good questions?

#### A NOTE ON AI USE

Please note that only the AI assistant built into the HackerRank environment is permitted during this interview. External AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.) may not be used.

## **Technical Discussion**

We want to get a sense for how you lead impactful projects with other engineers. In this interview you'll facilitate a small group discussion about a technical project that you led. 

You should choose a project that you can discuss with a group of Stripe engineers and/or managers.

You should prepare 20 minutes of material, and expect to field around 20 minutes of questions from the team. While it’s not mandatory, we welcome the opportunity to display or share any prepared material that helps explain the project itself. You will be able to share your computer screen via Zoom. If you choose to use a virtual whiteboard, feel free to use this [resource](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1EvJAA8d5lxQLbgP_-CDVFLw6E6oCZQLaqAQYoEw1AIc/edit?usp=sharing) to familiarize yourself with and get setup using Zoom or the Whimsical collaboration tools.

#### HOW TO PREPARE

Choose an example of your work that you’re particularly proud of, that you think speaks to your abilities as a technical and project leader. Please ensure the project you present has come to fruition.

* We’d love to see something that demonstrates your ability to handle projects with significant scope, complexity, or scale.   
* We value your ability to manage situations that involve technical, organizational, and people challenges. Your project discussion should speak to each of these areas.  
* Be specific wherever possible, including using technical details.  
* Speak to the work you personally did as well as the work you supported on the team.   
* The group of Stripes you’re with will primarily be experienced engineers and managers (though not necessarily in your area of expertise), so choosing more advanced technical topics will make for the most interesting discussion. Consider your audience to be smart, experienced generalists who like to learn new things. Since your audience may not be experienced in the tools/technologies you may have used \-- please err on the side of technical topics which will make for the most interesting discussion, but be sure to reserve time to give background and explain technical concepts. The reasons and tradeoffs behind why you chose those technologies is important for us to understand.  
* Since you will be presenting to an audience outside your current company, please avoid using any internal jargon and take time to explain any company specific acronyms or naming if it comes up.  
* Even though you’re wonderful, we know high-impact projects can get bumpy, and we love hearing about this side of your work, too.

# **Frequently Asked Questions**

### How are video interviews different from in-person interviews? Is there anything special I should know?

We understand that interviewing virtually is a different experience than interviewing in person. While some candidates may prefer this format, others may have preferred in-person interviews. We appreciate your flexibility with this\! 

Your interviews will use our video conferencing tool, Zoom. There are a few things you should know about interviewing over Zoom:

### Wondering what to wear?

Many people at Stripe show up to work in jeans and a t-shirt (or button down/blouse). Feel free to wear what you're most accustomed to wearing, and we promise no one will judge you for it\!

### Why is there more than one interviewer in my interview?

You may notice that your interview has more than one Stripe team member on your Zoom. This is to ensure we are holding a consistent hiring bar, and to make sure we have enough fully trained interviewers to help us scale. The additional folks on the Zoom are shadowing the interview, which is part of our interviewer training process. You will work directly with your interviewer, who is fully trained, while another Stripe team member will observe the process to make sure they can replicate it fairly and consistently.
