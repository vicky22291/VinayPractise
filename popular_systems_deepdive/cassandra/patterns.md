# Cross-System Patterns

<!-- nav:start -->
**[Index](README.md)** · [Cassandra 00 Overview](cassandra-00-overview.md) · Sibling catalogues: [Kubernetes](../kubernetes/patterns.md) · [Kafka](../kafka/patterns.md)
<!-- nav:end -->

A running catalogue of recurring distributed-systems patterns, the mechanism behind each, and which studied systems use it. Seeded from the Kubernetes deep dives (`systems/kubernetes-*.md`); extended with Cassandra (`systems/cassandra-*.md`). Extend a row rather than adding a duplicate pattern when a new system is added.

**Systems covered so far:** Kubernetes (all subsystems), etcd, **Cassandra 5.0 (all subsystems)**. Entries under *Elsewhere* for systems not yet deep-dived are pointers for future reports, not verified findings from this project.

**Why these two first.** Kubernetes and Cassandra sit at opposite ends of the coordination axis. Kubernetes routes every write through one Raft group and gets strict serialisability with a 5,000-node ceiling; Cassandra coordinates on nothing and gets unbounded scale with no global ordering. Reading a pattern across both columns usually shows the same problem solved by *adding* coordination or by *deferring* it — and what each choice costs.

## 1. Consensus and replication

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Raft consensus** | Leader-based log replication; entries committed once a quorum acks; leader elected by randomized timeouts | etcd — the entire cluster state sits behind one Raft group (K8s [report 01](../kubernetes/kubernetes-01-apiserver-etcd.md)) | **Absent by design.** No leader anywhere on the data path. CEP-21's CMS (6.0) introduces a linearizable metadata log, appended via Cassandra's own Paxos rather than Raft (C* [report 09](cassandra-09-tcm-accord.md)) | Consul, TiKV, CockroachDB, Kafka KRaft, Spanner (Paxos per split) |
| **Quorum reads (ReadIndex)** | Leader confirms it is still leader via a heartbeat round before serving a read, avoiding a log write | etcd linearizable reads; kube-apiserver uses them when `resourceVersion=""` | **Quorum without a leader.** `R + W > RF` guarantees set intersection with no leader to confirm anything (C* [report 05](cassandra-05-coordinator-consistency.md)). Cheaper, and gives correct reads rather than linearizable ones | Consul consistent reads, Zookeeper `sync()`, Spanner reads at TrueTime |
| **Single Raft group as a scaling ceiling** | All writes serialize through one leader; capacity is one node's fsync throughput | etcd, hence the 5,000-node envelope | **No ceiling of this kind.** Every partition is independent; write throughput scales linearly with nodes. The cost is that no cross-partition invariant is expressible (C* reports 02, 05) | Contrast: Spanner, CockroachDB and TiKV shard into many Raft groups; Kafka partitions per topic |
| **Leader election as an optimisation, not a lock** | Lease renewed before expiry; no fencing token validated by the resource being guarded | `coordination.k8s.io/Lease` for kube-controller-manager, kube-scheduler, CSI sidecars. A stalled leader can still write | **No leaders to elect.** The nearest analogue is the counter *leader replica*, chosen per request, which serialises the read-modify-write — and is exactly why counters are the one non-idempotent write (C* [report 02](cassandra-02-write-path.md)) | Chubby and Zookeeper *do* issue fencing tokens; HDFS NameNode uses epoch numbers |
| **Leaderless consensus with timestamp ordering** | Assign globally unique timestamps; conflicting operations execute in timestamp order on every replica; no leader to fail over | — | **Accord (CEP-15), 6.0.** HLC timestamps + Reorder Buffer for 1-RTT under geo-distribution + Fast Path Electorates to keep the fast path under failure (C* [report 09](cassandra-09-tcm-accord.md)) | Caesar, Tempo (the protocols CEP-15 critiques); EPaxos |
| **Per-partition consensus as an opt-in escape hatch** | Expensive coordination available for the minority of operations that need it, syntactically visible | — | **LWT/Paxos.** 4 round trips, `cas_contention_timeout: 1000ms`, single partition only, and silently broken by any non-LWT write to the same partition (C* reports 02, 05) | MongoDB transactions; DynamoDB conditional writes; the general "make the expensive thing opt-in" principle |

**Key judgement.** Kubernetes accepted a single-Raft-group ceiling in exchange for a strictly serialisable, watchable, whole-cluster keyspace. Cassandra accepted no global ordering in exchange for unbounded linear write scaling. **CEP-21 is Cassandra conceding that the *metadata* half of that bet was wrong** — ownership and schema need a linearizable log even when data does not. That decomposition (consensus for metadata, none for data) is the reusable lesson, and Kafka reached it independently with KRaft.

## 2. Storage engines and on-disk structures

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Write-ahead log** | Append durably, then apply to the in-memory/paged structure; replay on restart | etcd WAL, fsynced before Raft commit (K8s [report 01](../kubernetes/kubernetes-01-apiserver-etcd.md)) | **CommitLog.** 32MiB memory-mapped segments, shared across all tables, CRC per record. `commitlog_sync: periodic` acks *before* fsync — up to 10s of acked writes lost on power failure (C* [report 01](cassandra-01-storage-engine.md)) | Postgres WAL, Kafka segments, InnoDB redo log, RocksDB WAL |
| **B+tree page store** | Copy-on-write B+tree with a freelist; random reads cheap, writes amplify | etcd's bbolt backend; needs `defrag` to return space | **Not used.** The deliberate opposite choice | Postgres/InnoDB heaps+indexes |
| **LSM tree** | Buffer writes in memtables, flush to sorted files, compact in the background | *Not used by etcd* | **The whole storage engine.** Memtable → immutable SSTable → background compaction. This is the canonical open-source LSM after RocksDB (C* reports 01, 04) | RocksDB, HBase, ScyllaDB, TiKV, BigTable |
| **Trie as an index structure** | Byte-comparable keys share prefixes; lookup is O(key length) with no sampling and no heap residency | — | **Both memtable and SSTable index in 5.0, both opt-in.** `TrieMemtable` (CEP-19) shards by token with one writer per shard; BTI (CEP-25) replaces `Summary.db`+`Index.db` with `Partitions.db`+`Rows.db` tries (C* [report 01](cassandra-01-storage-engine.md)) | Adaptive radix trees in HyPer/DuckDB; ART in modern in-memory stores |
| **Sampled, heap-resident index as a density ceiling** | An index that must fit in memory forces coarser sampling as data grows, lengthening scans | — | **`Summary.db` in the `big` format.** The structural reason node density was capped, and precisely what BTI removes (C* reports 01, 10) | Any memory-resident sparse index; the general fix is always a trie or a paged structure |
| **MVCC with a monotonic revision** | Every mutation gets a global revision; old versions readable until compacted | etcd revisions exposed as `resourceVersion`; the watch protocol depends on them | **No MVCC and no snapshot isolation.** Reconciliation is per-cell last-write-wins on a microsecond timestamp, ties broken by value bytes. Order-independence is what makes replay safe (C* [report 01](cassandra-01-storage-engine.md)) | Postgres xmin/xmax, Spanner timestamps, CockroachDB MVCC |
| **Compaction vs reclamation as separate steps** | Logical version removal ≠ physical space return | etcd `compact` then `defrag`; forgetting the second causes `database space exceeded` | **Tombstone eligible ≠ tombstone dropped.** Past `gc_grace_seconds` (864000) a tombstone is *eligible*; it is dropped only if no non-input SSTable could hold the shadowed data — which under STCS may be never (C* reports 03, 04) | Postgres VACUUM vs VACUUM FULL |
| **Hard size quota with an alarm** | Refuse writes rather than degrade unboundedly | etcd `--quota-backend-bytes`, 2 GiB default, NOSPACE alarm puts the cluster read-only | **`cdc_total_space`** rejects writes to CDC tables when full; `commitlog_total_space` forces flushes rather than refusing. `disk_failure_policy: stop` takes the node out instead of degrading (C* reports 01, 02) | Kafka log retention, S3 bucket quotas |
| **Immutable files + reference-counted deletion** | Never modify a written file; delete only when no reader holds it | — | **SSTables.** Only `Statistics.db` is mutable. `LogTransaction` `*.log` files make compaction crash-consistent (C* reports 01, 04) | Git objects, S3 object versions, RocksDB SSTs |

## 3. Control and coordination

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Level-triggered reconciliation** | Act on current observed state, not on the delivered event; loops are idempotent and convergent | The core model — every controller, the kubelet syncLoop, kube-proxy's rule sync | **Anti-entropy is level-triggered; the data path is not.** Repair compares current state (Merkle trees) rather than replaying events, and is safe to rerun. Hints and read repair are the same idea at different latencies (C* [report 07](cassandra-07-repair-streaming.md)) | Terraform plan/apply, ArgoCD, AWS ASGs |
| **Declarative desired state + observed status** | Split intent from reality | Every Kubernetes object; `generation` vs `observedGeneration` | **Absent.** Cassandra has no desired-state model; the ring *is* the state. This is what makes topology changes imperative and dangerous, and what CEP-21 partially rectifies by making them log entries (C* reports 06, 09) | Crossplane, Cluster API |
| **Optimistic concurrency control** | Compare-and-swap on a version; retry on conflict; never lock | `resourceVersion` on every write; etcd `Txn` with `ModRevision`; HTTP 409 | **LWT `IF` conditions** are the only CAS available, and only per-partition. Ordinary writes have no conflict detection at all — concurrent writers both succeed and timestamps decide (C* reports 02, 05) | DynamoDB conditional writes, S3 preconditions, Git refs |
| **Watch + resume token** | Long-lived stream of changes keyed by a position | Kubernetes watch with `resourceVersion`; `410 Gone` forces a relist | **CDC is the closest thing and it is not a stream API.** Commitlog segments are hard-linked into `cdc_raw_directory`; consumption is the operator's problem, and a stalled consumer fills `cdc_total_space` and blocks writes (C* [report 02](cassandra-02-write-path.md)) | Kafka consumer offsets, DynamoDB Streams, Postgres logical replication slots |
| **Epoch stamped on every message to detect staleness** | Attach a monotonic position to each RPC so a stale participant is detectable rather than silently wrong | `resourceVersion` in watch and CAS | **The specific hole in 5.0, closed in 6.0.** Gossip-propagated ring views can diverge with no detection; CEP-21 puts the metadata epoch on every internode message (C* reports 06, 09) | Raft terms, Kafka leader epochs, HDFS NameNode epochs, view numbers in viewstamped replication |
| **Expectations / in-flight accounting** | Controller records what it just asked for so it does not re-issue before the cache catches up | `UIDTrackingControllerExpectations` in the ReplicaSet controller | **Hints are the analogue**, but coordinator-side and for a peer rather than for itself: a durable record of "this replica owes me a write", TTL `max_hint_window: 3h` (C* [report 05](cassandra-05-coordinator-consistency.md)) | Any actor-model outstanding-request table; TCP's in-flight window |

## 4. Caching and staleness

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Read-through cache in front of the datastore** | One reader populates a shared cache; many consumers served from RAM | apiserver watch cache | **Key cache** (`min(5% heap, 100MiB)`) maps (sstable, key) → offset, skipping the index entirely — the cheapest win in the system (C* [report 03](cassandra-03-read-path.md)) | CDN origin shielding, DB read replicas |
| **Client-side replicated cache** | Every consumer maintains a full local mirror via list+watch | client-go informers in every component | **Every node's local `TokenMetadata` and schema**, populated by gossip. Same shape, weaker consistency — and the weakness is the bug CEP-21 fixes (C* reports 06, 09) | Envoy xDS state-of-the-world, Consul agent caches |
| **Deliberately stale cache with a safety backstop** | Accept staleness, then revalidate where being wrong is expensive | Scheduler snapshot is stale → kubelet re-runs admission | **Digest reads.** One replica sends data, the rest send MD5s; a mismatch escalates to full reads plus a repair write. Cheap when consistent, a latency cliff when not (C* [report 03](cassandra-03-read-path.md)) | TLB + page fault; conditional GET |
| **Cache invalidation cost driving a feature off by default** | A cache whose invalidation granularity is wrong is worse than no cache | — | **`row_cache_size: 0MiB`.** The row cache holds a whole partition, so any write to it invalidates everything. Off by default and should usually stay off (C* [report 03](cassandra-03-read-path.md)) | Any coarse-grained cache; the general lesson is that invalidation granularity, not hit rate, decides viability |
| **Let the OS page cache do the work** | Keep the application's own cache small and leave RAM to the kernel | — | **The standard Cassandra sizing rule.** Heap under ~31 GB for compressed oops; the remainder to page cache, which is a better read cache than any heap structure (C* [report 10](cassandra-10-scale-operations.md)) | Kafka's design explicitly; Varnish; SQLite |
| **Relist storms / thundering herd** | Many clients simultaneously lose position and re-read everything | apiserver restart → every informer relists | **Hint replay storms.** A returning node is hit by every peer's accumulated hints at once — which is exactly why `hinted_handoff_throttle: 1024KiB` is deliberately slow (C* [report 05](cassandra-05-coordinator-consistency.md)) | Cache stampede on TTL expiry |

## 5. Flow control and back-pressure

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fair queuing with shuffle sharding** | Hash each flow into a random subset of queues so one heavy flow rarely collides with any given light one | API Priority and Fairness: FlowSchema → PriorityLevel → shuffle-sharded queues + seats | **Absent.** Cassandra has bounded per-stage queues and drops, with no fairness between tenants or flows — a real gap for multi-tenant deployments (C* [report 02](cassandra-02-write-path.md)) | AWS shuffle sharding, Linux SFQ, Google's Slicer |
| **Admission shedding before expensive work** | Reject or queue at the cheapest point in the pipeline | APF runs *before* authorization in the handler chain | **Guardrails reject at parse/execute time**: `tombstone_failure_threshold: 100000`, `batch_size_fail_threshold: 50KiB`, `sai_*` term limits. `native_transport_max_concurrent_requests_in_bytes` sheds at the front door (C* reports 03, 10) | Envoy admission control, TCP SYN cookies |
| **Bounded stage queues that drop rather than block** | Overflow is discarded so upstream stages stay responsive | — | **SEDA-derived stages.** `concurrent_writes: 32`, `concurrent_reads: 32`; overflow increments `Dropped MUTATION` in `tpstats`. **A dropped mutation is a silently lost replica copy that only repair fixes** (C* [report 02](cassandra-02-write-path.md)) | Classic SEDA; Disruptor ring buffers; the general risk is that dropping is invisible |
| **Client-side rate limiting** | Callers self-limit to protect a shared server | `--kube-api-qps` / `--kube-api-burst` on every component | **Driver-side**: token-aware routing, connection pooling, and — critically — client timeouts that must exceed server timeouts or retries pile on during a slow period (C* reports 05, 10) | gRPC client throttling, AWS SDK adaptive retry |
| **Background work throttled against the foreground** | Cap the rate of maintenance so it cannot starve the request path | — | **Three separate throttles**: `compaction_throughput: 64MiB/s`, `stream_throughput_outbound: 24MiB/s`, `hinted_handoff_throttle: 1024KiB`. All three are spinning-disk-era defaults that are commonly wrong now (C* reports 04, 07, 10) | InnoDB IO capacity; Kafka replication quotas; `ionice` |
| **Exponential backoff with a dedup queue** | Failures requeue with growing delay; duplicate keys collapse | client-go `workqueue`; scheduler backoffQ | **Paxos ballot retry** under `cas_contention_timeout: 1000ms` — and it is a *counter*-example: competing ballots cause mutual rejection, so contention degrades superlinearly rather than backing off gracefully (C* [report 02](cassandra-02-write-path.md)) | SQS visibility timeout + DLQ |

## 6. Failure detection and recovery

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lease-based liveness** | Renew a small object frequently; absence means "presumed dead" | Node Lease renewed every 10s in `kube-node-lease` | **Not leases — gossip heartbeats.** `HeartBeatState` with generation + version, exchanged at 1 Hz with 3 peers (C* [report 06](cassandra-06-membership-gossip.md)) | Chubby/Zookeeper sessions, Consul TTL checks |
| **Separate heartbeat from full status** | A cheap frequent signal plus an expensive infrequent one | Node Lease (tiny, 10s) vs full Node status (large, 5m) — what made 5,000-node clusters viable | **Gossip digests vs full `EndpointState` exchange.** The three-message SYN/ACK/ACK2 round sends digests first and only the states the peer lacks — bandwidth is O(changes), not O(cluster) (C* [report 06](cassandra-06-membership-gossip.md)) | BGP keepalives vs full route refresh |
| **Adaptive failure detection (φ accrual)** | Judge lateness against the observed arrival distribution rather than a fixed timeout | — | **`FailureDetector`, `phi_convict_threshold: 8`.** Suspicion on a log scale; adapts to each link's own jitter. **Conviction is a local, unilateral opinion — two nodes can permanently disagree** (C* [report 06](cassandra-06-membership-gossip.md)) | Akka cluster (same paper); Hayashibara et al. SRDS 2004 |
| **Detection of slowness separated from detection of death** | One mechanism for "is it there", another for "is it useful" | Readiness probes vs liveness probes; EndpointSlice removal vs pod restart | **φ-accrual detects death; the DynamicEndpointSnitch detects slowness** (`update_interval: 100ms`, `badness_threshold: 1.0`) and demotes without ejecting. **A slow node is worse than a dead one** because it stays UP and burns the timeout (C* reports 05, 06) | Load balancer health checks vs outlier detection (Envoy) |
| **Hedged requests / speculative retry** | Send a duplicate to another replica when the first exceeds a latency threshold | — | **`speculative_retry: 99p`** (table default). Buys tail latency with load; `ALWAYS` doubles read traffic (C* reports 03, 05) | Dean & Barroso *The Tail at Scale*; gRPC hedging; DynamoDB |
| **Rate-limited mass eviction** | Cap how fast a detected failure can cascade | node-lifecycle controller's eviction rate limits | **Hint delivery throttling** serves the same purpose from the other side: don't let a recovering node be overwhelmed by the recovery itself (C* [report 05](cassandra-05-coordinator-consistency.md)) | Circuit breakers; AWS ASG health-check grace |
| **Cooperative deletion with a deadline** | Removal blocked until every party has acked — or until a timer expires | `deletionTimestamp` + finalizers; **no deadline**, so one absent party wedges forever | **`gc_grace_seconds: 864000`.** Deletion in an eventually-consistent store is undecidable without a timer, so Cassandra picks 10 days and accepts resurrection if repair misses it (C* reports 03, 07) | Two-phase teardown in Terraform; the contrast is instructive — K8s waits forever, Cassandra guesses |

## 7. Anti-entropy and convergence *(new section — Cassandra's distinctive contribution)*

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Layered convergence at different latencies** | Several independent mechanisms, none sufficient alone, each covering what the faster one missed | — | **Hints (seconds–3h) → read repair (query-time, hot data) → Merkle repair (scheduled, all data).** Cold data would diverge forever on read repair alone; hints expire; repair is slow. All three are load-bearing (C* reports 05, 07) | Dynamo (the origin); Riak; any AP store |
| **Merkle tree difference detection** | Hash tree over a key range; compare roots, descend to differing leaves, transfer only those | — | **`ValidationCompaction` + `MerkleTrees`.** Tree depth bounded by `repair_session_space` (1/16 heap) — so **one differing row streams a whole leaf's token range** ("overstreaming"). The fix is smaller ranges, not deeper trees (C* [report 07](cassandra-07-repair-streaming.md)) | Dynamo, Riak, git, ZFS scrub, rsync's rolling checksums |
| **Read-time reconciliation with write-back** | Detect divergence during a normal read and fix it inline | — | **`read_repair: BLOCKING`** (table default). The client waits for the repair write — which is what converts `R + W > RF` from correct reads into *monotonic* reads (C* reports 03, 05) | Dynamo read repair; DNS cache warming is a weak analogue |
| **A deadline that makes deletion decidable** | You cannot forget a fact until everyone has heard it, and "everyone has heard it" needs a timer | — | **`gc_grace_seconds` + a repair schedule that provably fits inside it.** Violating the coupling causes silent data resurrection with no error, no metric and no alert (C* reports 03, 07, 10) | Tombstones in Dynamo, Riak, Redis CRDTs; NNTP expiry |
| **Preview / dry-run for a destructive maintenance operation** | Measure the work without doing it | `kubectl diff`, `--dry-run=server` | **`nodetool repair -prv`.** Reports divergence without streaming — the correct first step before tuning repair, and badly under-used (C* [report 07](cassandra-07-repair-streaming.md)) | `rsync --dry-run`; Terraform plan |
| **Whole-file transfer when the range matches exactly** | Skip deserialisation when the unit on disk happens to be the unit being moved | — | **Zero-copy / entire-SSTable streaming** (4.0+). Order-of-magnitude faster bootstrap; does not apply to fine-grained repair diffs (C* [report 07](cassandra-07-repair-streaming.md)) | `sendfile()`; Kafka's zero-copy consumer path |

## 8. Placement and partitioning

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Computed placement (no lookup)** | Derive location from the key itself, so no metadata service is on the request path | — (the scheduler *assigns*, and the assignment is stored) | **Consistent hashing.** `Murmur3Partitioner` token → walk the ring → RF nodes, computed locally with zero lookups. **The single biggest architectural difference from HBase, MongoDB and Kubernetes** (C* reports 00, 05) | Dynamo, Riak, Ceph CRUSH, memcached client hashing |
| **Assigned placement with a metadata lookup** | A registry says where things live | kube-scheduler writes a `Binding`; kubelet reads it | **Only for metadata, and only from 6.0** (CEP-21's CMS), and even then the log is materialised locally so the data path keeps computing (C* [report 09](cassandra-09-tcm-accord.md)) | HBase `hbase:meta`, MongoDB config servers, HDFS NameNode |
| **Virtual nodes to smooth ownership** | Many small ranges per physical node so additions stream from many peers in parallel | — | **`num_tokens: 16`** (was 256 pre-4.0). Fewer vnodes cut repair and streaming work ~16×; viable only because of the balance-optimising allocator, `allocate_tokens_for_local_replication_factor: 3` (C* [report 06](cassandra-06-membership-gossip.md)) | Dynamo's virtual nodes; Ceph placement groups |
| **Failure-domain-aware replica placement** | Spread replicas so one domain's loss costs at most one replica | Topology spread constraints, pod anti-affinity | **`NetworkTopologyStrategy`** walks the ring skipping racks already used — RF=3 over 3 racks gives one per rack, which is what makes `LOCAL_QUORUM` survive an AZ loss. **Silently fails if racks are unbalanced** (C* [report 05](cassandra-05-coordinator-consistency.md)) | S3 AZ placement; HDFS rack awareness |
| **Sharding a large collection into bounded units** | Split one hot object into many small ones to bound per-unit cost | EndpointSlice (100 endpoints per slice) replacing the single Endpoints object | **UCS shard-by-token-range** keeps SSTables near `target_sstable_size: 1GiB` regardless of node density; `sstable_growth: 0.333` sets how size trades against count (C* [report 04](cassandra-04-compaction.md)) | Kafka partitions; DynamoDB item limits |
| **The partition as the unit of everything** | One key determines storage locality, ordering, atomicity and the query surface simultaneously | — | **Cassandra's partition key.** It fixes placement, on-disk locality, the only atomicity scope, and which queries are expressible — and **`ALTER TABLE` can never change it** (C* [report 08](cassandra-08-cql-sai.md)) | Kafka partition key; DynamoDB partition key; the general lesson is that overloading one identifier this hard is powerful and unforgiving |

## 9. Extension and isolation

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Out-of-process plugin at the trust boundary** | Third-party code runs in its own process, reached over a narrow RPC | CRI, CNI, CSI, DRA device plugins | **Absent.** Everything runs in the one JVM: compaction strategies, memtables, index implementations, snitches, authorizers are all in-process classes (C* reports 01, 04) | Envoy ext_authz, Postgres FDW |
| **In-process pluggable implementation via an SPI** | Swap an algorithm by class name, same process | Scheduler plugins; CEL policy | **The Cassandra way.** `IPartitioner`, `IEndpointSnitch`, `AbstractCompactionStrategy`, `Memtable` (CEP-11), `SSTableFormat` (CEP-25), `IAuthorizer`, pluggable crypto provider (5.0) | JDBC drivers; Java `ServiceLoader` generally |
| **Interception hooks with a declared failure policy** | Extension points that block writes must declare what happens when unavailable | Admission webhooks' `failurePolicy` | **Failure policies on subsystems rather than hooks**: `commit_failure_policy: stop`, `disk_failure_policy: stop`. Same idea — name the behaviour when a dependency fails, don't leave it emergent (C* reports 01, 10) | Circuit-breaker fallbacks; fail-open vs fail-closed |
| **Derived data co-located with its source** | Store the derivative inside the same immutable unit as the original so it cannot diverge | — | **SAI (CEP-7).** Index components are part of the SSTable, written at flush and rebuilt at compaction — so index/data divergence is not a reachable state. **Contrast materialized views**, which write to a different replica set via a batchlog and *can* diverge permanently, hence `materialized_views_enabled: false` (C* [report 08](cassandra-08-cql-sai.md)) | Covering indexes; Lucene segments; the general lesson — co-location eliminates a consistency class rather than managing it |
| **Shipping a feature disabled by default** | Ship the code, withhold the endorsement | Feature gates in Alpha | **The defining pattern of 5.0.** Trie memtables, BTI, UCS all present and all opt-in; MVs, SASI, transient replication and compact storage all `false`. **Reading `cassandra.yaml` rather than the release announcement is the difference between knowing a system and knowing its marketing** (C* reports 00, 11) | Kubernetes alpha gates; Chrome flags; LaunchDarkly-style rollout |

## 10. Resource management and operations

| Pattern | Mechanism | In Kubernetes | In Cassandra | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Request/limit split** | One number for admission, another for enforcement | requests → scheduling + `cpu.weight`; limits → CFS quota and OOM kill | **Absent** — one JVM per node, no per-tenant or per-table resource isolation. A hot table starves others through the shared memtable pool (C* reports 01, 10) | Borg; JVM `-Xms`/`-Xmx`; cgroups |
| **Local pressure eviction ranked by contract** | The node sheds load itself, worst-behaved tenant first | kubelet eviction manager: rank by QoS, then usage over request | **Guardrails are the analogue, and they reject rather than rank**: kill the query that would hurt the node (tombstones, batch size, index count) rather than deciding whose work matters least (C* [report 10](cassandra-10-scale-operations.md)) | Load shedding in RPC servers; Linux OOM score |
| **Disruption budgets as a safety interlock** | Voluntary operations must ask permission | PDBs enforced by the Eviction API but not by node-pressure eviction | **Procedural, not enforced.** "One topology change at a time", "drain before restart", "no repair during a mixed-version window" are rules the system does not check — CEP-21 makes the first of them enforceable (C* reports 06, 09, 10) | Maintenance windows; Chubby's "at most N down" |
| **Recovery time as the real capacity constraint** | Sizing is bounded by how long it takes to rebuild, not by how much fits | Node count vs etcd/apiserver limits, not per-node recovery | **The governing constraint on node density.** A 10 TB node may store and serve fine and take three days to repair — which means it cannot be repaired inside a 10-day gc_grace if anything goes wrong twice (C* reports 07, 10) | RAID rebuild windows; Ceph backfill; the general principle in any replicated store |
| **Silent correctness debt with no error signal** | A violated invariant that produces no exception, no latency change and no metric | Stuck finalizers are at least visible in the object | **Repair debt.** Missing the gc_grace deadline produces resurrected data days later, indistinguishable from an application bug. **Time-since-successful-repair per node is the alert nobody has** (C* reports 07, 10) | Backup that was never restore-tested; certificate expiry; silent data corruption without scrubbing |
| **Conservative defaults outliving their hardware** | Values calibrated for one era shipped unchanged into the next | — | `compaction_throughput: 64MiB/s`, `stream_throughput_outbound: 24MiB/s`, `commitlog_sync_period: 10000ms` — all spinning-disk/1GbE-era and commonly 1–2 orders of magnitude wrong on NVMe (C* reports 04, 07, 10) | Linux `vm.swappiness`; JVM heap defaults; TCP initial congestion window |

## Open questions to revisit as more systems are added

- **Where else does "level-triggered wins" hold, and where does it fail?** It requires cheap full-state reads. Cassandra's answer is instructive: it is level-triggered for *anti-entropy* (Merkle comparison of current state) and edge-triggered for the *data path* (fire-and-forget mutations). So the boundary is not "control plane vs data plane" but "can you afford to read the whole state?" — repair can, a write cannot.
- **Fencing tokens.** Kubernetes has none and relies on idempotence. Cassandra also has none in 5.0 — and **that is exactly the CEP-21 bug**, since topology changes are *not* idempotent the way reconciliation is. The emerging rule: idempotence substitutes for fencing only when the operation genuinely is idempotent, and ownership changes never are. Compare against Chubby, HDFS epochs and Kafka's producer epoch when those reports exist.
- **Single Raft group vs sharded consensus vs no consensus.** We now have both extremes: etcd's one group (strict serialisability, 5,000-node ceiling) and Cassandra's none (linear scale, no ordering). Spanner and CockroachDB sit between with per-range consensus. Quantify what the middle actually costs operationally — that is the missing report.
- **B+tree vs LSM for a metadata store.** etcd chose bbolt; Cassandra is the reference LSM. Compare against ZooKeeper (in-memory + snapshots) and TiKV (RocksDB) on read latency, write amplification and compaction pauses.
- **Does every eventually-consistent store converge on the same three-layer anti-entropy?** Cassandra and Dynamo/Riak share hints + read repair + Merkle repair almost exactly. Is that the unique stable design, or an artefact of shared ancestry? Check against Ceph and Azure Storage when those reports exist.
- **When is co-locating derived data with its source strictly better than maintaining it separately?** SAI vs materialized views is the cleanest natural experiment in this project so far — same team, same system, one design that cannot diverge and one that provably does. Look for the same contrast in Lucene segments vs external indexes, and in Kubernetes' `status` subresource vs separate reporting objects.
- **Which of Cassandra's "procedural guarantees" should be enforced by the system?** "One topology change at a time" became enforceable with CEP-21. "Repair inside gc_grace" became schedulable with CEP-37. What remains procedural — no repair during upgrades, drain before restart, client timeout > server timeout — and which of those is a missing feature rather than an operator's job?
